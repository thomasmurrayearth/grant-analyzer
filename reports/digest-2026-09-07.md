## 4. Anything unusual

**a) Two of three finished analyses were never seen.**
This is the standout finding. The app records a `results_rendered` event when
results actually appear on screen. This week there were three completed runs and
**one** `results_rendered` event (for the second Notpla run). The first Notpla
run and the Buraanbur Sahlan run both finished properly in the database but
never rendered in anyone's browser.

The most likely explanation is simply the wait: runs take 20–28 minutes, and
people close the tab. You spent about $1.22 on two reports nobody read.

**b) Nobody downloaded a real XLSX — but you can't actually tell.**
There were zero download events this week. However, when I checked the frontend
code, the "Download XLSX" button only records an event when it's the *sample*
report (`frontend/index.html:1808–1827`). A real download from a real run isn't
tracked at all. So "zero downloads" here means "not measured", not "didn't
happen". That blind spot sits right on top of the question you most want
answered: does anyone take the deliverable away?

**c) Results are inconsistent for identical input.**
The two Notpla runs used the same website and the same settings an hour apart
and returned 10 opportunities and 5 opportunities. Since `SHORTLIST_SIZE` is 10,
the first run filled every available slot and the second filled half. That's a
big swing for identical input, and it's consistent with the wider history (runs
have returned anywhere from 1 to 10).

**d) The benchmark job is spamming the events table.**
There were 24 events this week, and **12 of them** — half — are `benchmark_cycle`
rows written every 30 minutes on 1 September, each saying "skipped: cycle already
handled". They're harmless but they crowd out the real funnel events and will
make future digests noisier as they accumulate.

**e) No feedback, no waitlist sign-ups.** Both tables were empty this week.

## Ideas to improve the app

### 1. Track the real XLSX download (small fix, big blind spot)
Add a `track('xlsx_download')` call to the real-download branch of the button
handler in `frontend/index.html` (around line 1817, the `fetch('/download')`
path), alongside the `sample_xlsx_download` call that already exists for the
sample. Right now the single most important question — *do people keep the
report?* — is unanswerable from your data. This is a one-line change and it
makes every future digest more useful.

### 2. Make the 25-minute wait survivable
Two of three finished reports were never seen, and someone re-ran the same
company rather than waiting. The push-notification plumbing already exists
(`main.py:367` sends "Found N opportunities. Tap to view"), so the gap is
probably that people don't turn notifications on, or can't get back to a result
after closing the tab. Two cheap options: (a) prompt for notification permission
at the moment the analysis starts, when the user has just accepted a long wait,
and (b) show a "your results will stay at this link" URL up front, so a closed
tab isn't a lost report. Results already survive restarts via
`db.get_completed_result`, so the recovery path exists — it just isn't offered
to the user.

### 3. Drop or rethink the settings nobody uses
Across all 33 runs ever recorded, the geographies box has been filled in
**once** (3%), and the consortium / accelerators / prizes tick-boxes have been
left at their defaults **every single time**. They're taking up space on the
form and contributing nothing to the analysis. Either remove them from the first
screen (and put them behind an "advanced" toggle), or — more interesting — make
geography something the app *infers* from the company profile it already builds
and then shows back for confirmation, so it gets used instead of skipped.
