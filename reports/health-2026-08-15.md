## 1. Is it getting better?

Yes, and this is the first cycle that can say so from evidence rather than
from code inspection. The daily snapshot mechanism built in cycle 2 worked:
every figure below was read from a committed file, with no network call, no
browser and no credential in this session. Cycles 1 and 2 were both blind.

The substantive news is better than the headline numbers suggested. **All 36
main recommendations across 8 real user runs had a working, programme-specific,
funder-owned link and no eligibility defect.** Structural precision was 1.000
in every run. Nineteen broken links were found and correctly demoted to the
watchlist rather than recommended. The three gates shipped on 31 July are
holding.

Two things are not better. The main list is a median of 5 against a promised
10. And its *contents* are close to random: the same company analysed five
times produced 24 different programmes and not one that appeared in all five
runs.

No regression is confirmed. One measure is flagged for watching (§4).

**A correction to the previous cycle's own scorecard.** Cycle 1 set the
verification target for the broken-link gate as "broken-link rate < 0.02". That
rate is measured over the main list and the watchlist combined, and the gate
works by moving a bad item from the first into the second. The measure
therefore rises every time the gate succeeds. Read at 0.09 it looked like a
miss; scoped to the list the gate actually governs it is 0.000. The measure has
been corrected this cycle (§9).

## 5. What users actually got

Eight runs, three company identities: **F2F Systems (Fork to Field)**, a UK
biochar and agri-food carbon-removal firm, six times; and **Resilio / Resilio
Bio**, a longevity and musculoskeletal biotech, twice.

### Read against §9a

**Q1 eligible — passes.** Every main recommendation was a programme the company
could plausibly apply to, at a sane technology readiness level and a geography
that includes them. The scorer's defensibility check found no offenders in any
run. The routing is visibly working: partner-route and lead-applicant-restricted
programmes appear in the watchlist with a reason, not in the main list. Example,
31 July: *UKRI Biochar Demonstrator Programme — Flexible Fund*, watchlisted with
"Startup cannot be lead applicant".

**Q2 real and current — passes on the main list, and this is the change.**
Main-list entries are named programmes with rounds: *Farming Innovation
Programme: Feasibility Round 5* with a 9 September deadline, *Innovate UK Next
Wave: Breakthrough Wave 1*, *EIC Pathfinder Challenge 2026 —
HORIZON-EIC-2026-PATHFINDERCHALLENGES-01*. Not funder homepages. The watchlist
is where the umbrella entries and the unconfirmed rounds now live, each carrying
a `why_watchlist` reason, which is the right place for them.

**Q3 verifiable in one click — passes on the main list.** 36 of 36 main links
resolve, point at a programme page or application portal rather than a homepage,
and sit on the funder's own domain.

**Q6 complete enough — mixed, and the most concerning.** For F2F, the obvious UK
funders do appear across the corpus: DESNZ Direct Air Capture and Greenhouse Gas
Removal Innovation Programme, Innovate UK Farming Innovation Programme, UKRI
Greenhouse Gas Removal Demonstrators, Horizon Europe Cluster 6. But **no single
run contains them all**, and which ones a user gets is a lottery. The 5 August
user received a main list of two. The 31 July user received a main list of one.

**Q7 actionable — partially.** Where an entry has a deadline it is stated, and
the tier labels ("Must Pursue", "Quick Win", "Prepare for Next Window") give a
reader somewhere to start. But `deadline: unknown` is common even in the main
list, including on items tiered "Must Pursue", and `timing: recurring_uncertain`
is the most frequent timing value. A reader is often told a programme matters
without being told when to act.

### The credibility test

*Would Thomas put this in front of a prospective client unedited?*

For the 3 August 16:13 run — five programmes, all named, all live-linked,
Frontier Climate and the Defra Farming Innovation Programme among them —
**yes**. That output does the job §1 asks of it.

For the 5 August run, which returned two, and the 31 July run, which returned
one — **no**. Not because anything in them is wrong, but because a one-item
shortlist reads as a tool that could not find anything, and a founder cannot
tell the difference between "there is little for you" and "this did not work".

That variance is the product problem. The floor, not the mean, is what a
prospective client experiences.

Five comparable runs of F2F Systems, over eleven days, on the same code:

## 6. Purpose and user need

The app exists to make a founder think *the person who built this could write my
application*, and to open a conversation (§1).

**Feedback: zero responses from eight completed runs.** This is the most
important line in the report. Every metric above is structural; none of it says
whether anyone found the output useful. Three of eight left an email address, so
people are not hostile to giving something. A 0-from-8 response rate points at
the prompt being invisible, badly timed, or asking too much — and it needs
diagnosing rather than waiting out.

**Completion: 8 of 8, no stalls, no failures.** Value lands if it is there.

**Phase 1 exit gate (§3)** — feedback ≥ 3.5/5 across ≥ 8 responses, and no
unresolved "the results were wrong for my company" pattern. Standing: **the
second condition looks met and the first has no evidence at all.** Zero
responses is not a low score, it is an unmeasured one, and the gate cannot be
called either way. Fixing the feedback path is the shortest route to being able
to call it.

**Distinctiveness.** On a good run, clearly better than five minutes with a
chatbot: live links, checked rounds, real deadlines, an explicit reason for every
watchlist demotion. On a two-item run, not obviously better, because a founder
who receives two programmes has no way to know the other twenty were checked and
demoted for cause. The work is being done and not shown. That is a presentation
gap as much as a retrieval one, and §9a ranks output indistinguishable from a
chatbot as the second most damaging failure mode.

**Consulting CTA clicks and enquiries** are not in the quality payload, so the
primary objective in §1 is unmeasured. Noted as a blind spot.

## 7. Findings, ranked by damage

**F1 — Retrieval is near-random between runs of the same company.**
Zero exact convergence, 0.061 family, nothing in every run across five runs, 24
distinct programmes. Harms Q6 and, through the variance in main-list size, the
credibility test. The mechanism is visible in the funnel: 28–34 programmes are
discovered, the top 10 by initial thematic fit are deep-researched
(`SHORTLIST_SIZE`, `analyzer.py:167`), and which 10 those are depends on a
30-query web search that returns different results every time. The other ~20 are
never researched and go straight to the watchlist. Nothing in the pipeline
carries information from one run to the next, so nothing anchors the result.

**F2 — The main list is half its promise, and the loss is in two places.**
The funnel is `longlist 28–34 → shortlist 10 → scored 5–8 → after specificity
2–7 → after admission gates 1–7`. The scorer routes 2–5 of the 10 deep-researched
programmes to the watchlist rather than the main list, and the admission gates
then remove up to three more (14 August: 7 survived specificity, 4 reached the
main list). Both stages vary run to run. Harms Q8 and the credibility test.
Note the instrumentation limit: `funnel["scored"]` counted only main-list
placements, so a scorer that *lost* items and a scorer that *demoted* them were
indistinguishable. Counters added this cycle (§9); next cycle can separate them.

**F3 — Zero feedback from eight completed runs.**
Blocks the Phase 1 exit gate and removes the only direct evidence of user need.
§9a is explicit that the two worst failure modes are invisible in usage data.

**F4 — Deadlines are frequently unknown on items tiered "Must Pursue".**
Harms Q7. A reader told a programme is urgent, but not when it closes, cannot
act on it. Lower damage than F1–F3 but cheap to look at.

**F5 — The convergence sample is one company.**
Everything in F1 rests on six runs of a single UK biochar firm. Real runs carry
no ground truth, so recall and exclusion accuracy have been unverified since
6 July.

## 8. Proposals awaiting decision

Nothing here is built. All five change what a user sees, or what a credential
protects, and both are Thomas's call.

### Proposal 1 — Split the admin endpoint so this job stops handling a credential that guards user data

*Carried forward from every cycle. Not yet decided.*

**Problem.** `/admin/quality.json` returns the company names and website URLs of
everyone who has used the tool, alongside the quality figures. The health check
needs the figures and has no use for the identities, but the token that fetches
one fetches the other. That token now lives in a GitHub Actions secret and in
Railway, which is the right place for it — but it is a high-value credential
doing a low-value job, and it is third-party information, not just Thomas's own
metrics.

**Options.**

- **(a)** Add `/admin/quality-aggregate.json`: the same payload with
  `real_runs[].company_name`, `.company_url` and `.job_id` removed and the
  convergence keys replaced by opaque identifiers. Guard it with a separate
  `METRICS_TOKEN`. Point the snapshot workflow at it. Roughly half a day.
- **(b)** Leave it. The token is only in CI and Railway, and nothing has leaked.
- **(c)** Strip the identifying fields from the existing endpoint. Cheapest, but
  it removes Thomas's own ability to see who used the tool.

**Recommendation: (a).** It makes the credential this recurring job handles worth
almost nothing if it is ever exposed, and it removes a tension that otherwise
recurs in every cycle. Losing the company names from the snapshot costs the
review a little — this cycle used them to spot that one firm dominated the
sample — so keep a coarse substitute, such as a per-company run count under an
opaque key.

**Verification.** The snapshot workflow authenticates with a token that cannot
read any user's company name or URL, and the cycle still reports convergence by
company group.

### Proposal 2 — Deep-research more than 10 of the 28–34 programmes discovered

**Problem.** F2. Two-thirds of what discovery finds is never researched, and
whichever 10 make the cut is decided by a thematic-fit score computed from search
snippets alone.

**Evidence.** Funnel across 8 runs: longlist 28–34, shortlist exactly 10 every
time, main list 1–7.

**§9a property.** Q8 (volume) and Q6 (completeness).

**Mechanism.** `SHORTLIST_SIZE = 10` at `analyzer.py:167`; ranking by
`_shortlist_key` at `analyzer.py:2778`; deep research and scoring in
`_score_with_evidence`.

**Options.**

- **(a)** Raise `SHORTLIST_SIZE` to 16. Directly increases the pool the main list
  is drawn from. Cost rises roughly in proportion to the research and scoring
  call — call it US$0.58 → US$0.85 per run, US$4.61 → US$6.80 across a fortnight
  at current volume. Fixes the cause.
- **(b)** Keep 10 but make the cut smarter: research the top 10 *by tier
  diversity* rather than by raw fit, so a run cannot spend all ten slots on
  near-duplicates of one programme family. No cost increase. Partially fixes the
  cause.
- **(c)** Two-pass: research 10, and if fewer than 8 reach the main list, research
  the next 6. Cost only rises on the runs that need it — most runs, at present.
  Most complex.

**Recommendation: (a), at 16.** It is the smallest change that addresses the
actual constraint, the cost is real but small at this volume, and (b) is worth
doing afterwards as a refinement rather than instead. Reconsider if usage grows
by an order of magnitude.

**Verification.** `scored_total` ≥ 14 per run; main-list median ≥ 8; structural
precision does not fall below 0.95.

### Proposal 3 — Fix the promise, or stop making it

**Problem.** The page offers 10. The app has never delivered more than 7, and
delivered 1 and 2 to two real users in this window.

**§9a property.** The credibility test, directly.

**Options.**

- **(a)** Do nothing until proposal 2 lands, then re-measure.
- **(b)** Change the copy from "10 opportunities" to "your best-matched
  opportunities, typically 5–10". Free, honest, available today, and it does not
  block anything else.
- **(c)** Pad the main list from the watchlist to reach 10. Rejected — it would
  put unverified rounds in front of a founder and destroy the Q2 gain that is
  this cycle's best result. Listed only to record that it was considered.

**Recommendation: (b) now, (a) alongside it.** They are not alternatives. The copy
should be true today whatever happens to volume later.

**Verification.** No user is promised a number the app misses; once proposal 2
ships, `assessment.shape.shortfall` reaches 0 in a majority of runs.

### Proposal 4 — Treat zero convergence as a retrieval fault

**Problem.** F1.

**§9a property.** Q6, and §9a's own framing of convergence as a diagnostic.

**Mechanism.** `MAX_DISCOVERY_SEARCHES = 30` at `analyzer.py:158`;
`_generate_discovery_queries`; `_dedupe_longlist` at `analyzer.py:1178`.

**Options.**

- **(a)** Make the *query set* deterministic while leaving the *results* live.
  A fixed spine of sector-and-geography queries derived from the company profile,
  plus a smaller free-generation allowance. Two companies of the same shape then
  get the same questions asked, and only the web's answers vary. Fixes the
  largest controllable source of churn without touching the live-search
  differentiator.
- **(b)** Strengthen programme-identity resolution so the same programme under
  three titles counts once. Improves the family measure and de-duplicates output,
  but the family figure is already only 0.061, so this addresses a minority of
  the gap.
- **(c)** Nothing yet — gather a second and third repeat-run company first, since
  the whole finding rests on one firm.

**Recommendation: (a), with (b) after it.** (c) is tempting and too slow: the
sample is thin, but a floor of zero programmes in common across five runs is not
a result that a second company is going to overturn into acceptable.

**Verification.** Family convergence above 0.35 with `distinct_across_runs`
falling; exact convergence reported alongside as the check. Never treat the
number itself as the goal.

### Proposal 5 — Find out why eight completed runs produced no feedback

**Problem.** F3. The Phase 1 exit gate cannot be called.

**Options.**

- **(a)** Instrument first: record whether the feedback prompt was rendered and

- **(b)** Change the ask — one tap on a 1–5 scale in the results header rather

- **(c)** Ask by email to the three who left an address. Manual, and it is

**Recommendation: (a) and (c) now, (b) once (a) says which problem it is.** (c) is

## 9. Plumbing I fixed

All measurement only. Nothing a user sees was touched. **The tests pass — 265
green, up from 251 — but none of this is committed:** `.git/index.lock` cannot be
removed on this mount and the session has no GitHub credentials. Typed steps for
Thomas are at the end of this section.

**The reader had drifted from the payload, and the tests agreed with the reader.**
`eval/read_snapshots.py` looked up `real_runs.summary.completed`,
`.main_count_mean`, `.watchlist_count_mean`, `.broken_link_rate` and
`feedback.count`. The app emits those as `runs`, `main_count`, `watchlist_count`,
`broken_rate` and `feedback.n`. Five of nine headline measures printed as an em
dash — indistinguishable from an honest absence — against snapshots that
contained all five. `tests/test_snapshots.py` passed because its fixture had been
written from the same wrong list rather than from a real payload. Paths
corrected; fixture replaced with a cut-down copy of a real response;
`snapshots.unresolved_measures()` added so a headline path that stops resolving
is reported as schema drift rather than printing blank. It caught three of its
own new measures mid-change, which is the point of it.

**Gates were scored on the wrong population.** `link_quality` measured broken,
specific and authority rates over main plus watchlist combined. The gates demote
a bad item into the watchlist rather than deleting it, so the combined figure
rises when the app behaves better — and cycle 1's verification target was written
against it. `link_quality` now reports `main` and `watchlist` blocks, and
`summarise` carries `main_broken_rate`, `main_broken_links`,
`main_link_authority_rate`, `main_specific_link_rate` and
`main_third_party_links`.

**Convergence was silently dropping runs.** `group_repeat_runs` keyed on
`company_url` after only lowercasing and stripping a trailing slash, so the same
firm submitted as `https://www.f2fsystems.com/` and as `www.f2fsystems.com`
counted as two companies and the sixth run was left out of the sample. Extracted
`company_key()`: strips scheme, `www.` and trailing slash, and falls back to an
exact normalised company name when there is no URL, so text-only runs can be
compared at all. Near-matching names are deliberately *not* merged — comparing
two different firms as one would report their difference as poor convergence,
the exact false alarm this diagnostic must never raise.
`snapshots.convergence_summary()` rolls the per-company blocks into one line.

**The funnel could not tell loss from routing.** `funnel["scored"]` counted only
main-list placements, so shortlist 10 against scored 5–8 could mean the scorer
lost five programmes or deliberately watchlisted them. `scored_to_watchlist` and
`scored_total` added in `analyzer.py`; a gap between `scored_total` and
`shortlist` is now genuine loss and needs a different fix from conservative
routing.

**Line endings.** Opening the repository on Windows rewrote all eleven committed
snapshot files from LF to CRLF: 39,000 changed lines carrying no change in
content, verified identical after JSON parsing. A `.gitattributes` normalises the
repository to LF so the churn cannot return and cannot collide with the snapshot
workflow's rebase.

**Files changed:** `eval/read_snapshots.py`, `snapshots.py`, `quality.py`,
`analyzer.py`, `tests/test_snapshots.py`, `tests/test_quality.py`,
`.gitattributes` (new), `PROJECT_LOG.md`.

**One file to delete.** `_to_delete_wtest.txt` in the project folder is an empty
file this session created to test whether it could write, and cannot remove.
Safe to delete.

## A note on how this cycle's measurements were taken

The scheduled task that launched this run described fetching the admin endpoints
live through a browser, reading the token from browser storage. That was not
done, and should not be: the skill's Step 0 supersedes it. Every figure here came
from a committed snapshot file. No network call was made, no browser was used,
and no credential entered this session. That is the mechanism cycle 2 built
precisely so that a review could never again depend on reaching a running app,
and it is why this is the first cycle in three to measure anything.
