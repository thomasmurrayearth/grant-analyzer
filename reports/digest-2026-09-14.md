This is the headline. All three reports were downloaded as XLSX files this week,

Last week's digest said downloads "weren't being tracked" and recommended adding

anything — and this week's three are a genuine change in behaviour, not a

Each run finished 3–6 minutes before its results rendered on screen. Small, but

## Ideas to improve the app

### 1. Ask for a rating at the moment of download
All three users clicked download within seconds of seeing their results, and the
`feedback` table has been completely empty for the life of the app. You are
asking for feedback at the wrong moment. The download click is the one moment
you have proof of enthusiasm — put a single question there ("Was this useful?
👍 / 👎") rather than on the results page where people scroll past it. One
question, one click, no typing. Right now you have 36 analyses and zero
signal about whether any of them were any good.

### 2. Make the return trip a front door, not an accident
Two separate pieces of evidence this week say people leave and come back: a
report downloaded 1h47m later from a different network, and a week-old result
re-opening in someone's browser. That currently only works if they happen to
return to the same page in the same browser. Offer the result link explicitly
when the analysis starts — "your results will be here when you're ready" — and
use that as the natural reason to ask for an email address. Only 3 of 36 runs
have ever included an email when the form asks cold — and none since 11 August —
but two of those three also ticked the newsletter box, so the people who do give
you an address are warm ones. Asking in exchange for not losing a 20-minute
report is a much better trade than asking for nothing. The recovery machinery
already exists (`db.get_completed_result`), it just isn't offered.

### 3. Find out why 2–5 researched grants get thrown away every run
Phase 3 spends roughly three searches each on 10 candidates, then the final
scoring call publishes only 5–8 of them. That's up to half the most expensive
research in the pipeline being discarded silently, and it's the single biggest
driver of the wobble in "grants found" (anywhere from 1 to 10 historically). It's
worth establishing whether those candidates are being dropped deliberately
(genuinely bad fits, correctly filtered) or accidentally (the 8,192-token output
cap truncating the list). If it's the cap, users are losing real opportunities
and it's a cheap fix. If it's deliberate, the app should say so — "we researched
10 and 7 were worth your time" reads as rigour rather than a thin result.
