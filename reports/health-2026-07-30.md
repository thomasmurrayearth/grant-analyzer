| Measure | Value | What it means | §9a |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recall vs ground truth | 0.92 (range 0.8–1.0) | The known-right answers usually surface, but not every time | Q6 |
| Exclusion accuracy | 0.90 | **One run in five recommended a programme confirmed ineligible** | Q1 |
| Structural precision | 0.76 | Almost entirely broken application links | Q3 |
| Shortlist size | 4–5 | Against a promised 10 | Q8 |
| Watchlist size | 16.8 | Roughly four watchlist items per recommendation | Q8 |

| Convergence, exact / family | 0.21 / 0.25 | Two runs of the same company share about a fifth of their shortlist | Q5 |

Across all sixteen stored Thermify runs going back to May the picture is worse
— recall 0.78, exclusion accuracy 0.84, five runs in sixteen recommending
something confirmed ineligible — which is the June–July quality work showing up,
and the reason every figure is tracked as a trend rather than a verdict.

**On convergence.** The gap between exact-name and programme-family matching is
small (0.21 → 0.25). That matters: it means low agreement is *not* one programme
appearing under several names, which would be a de-duplication defect. It is
genuine retrieval churn. Per §9a this is a diagnostic pointing at discovery
quality, and the fix is better retrieval — never caching or pinning results,
which would re-serve a poor answer and destroy the live-search differentiator
the app is built on.

## Findings, ranked by damage

**1. Measurement is dead, silently.** (blocks everything)
The analytics database is unreachable and the app is designed not to complain.
Any feedback, funnel, cost and results data written since the project went away
is presumably gone with it. Mechanism: `db.py` swallows every write by design;
until today nothing checked the other direction. *Fixed as far as code can fix
it — see "Plumbing I fixed". Restoring the database is Thomas's action.*

**2. One run in five recommended a programme confirmed ineligible.**
(§9a failure mode 1 — output that can't be trusted)
Exclusion accuracy 0.90 across five runs; 0.84 across sixteen. This is the
defect a reader notices first and the one that most directly damages the
consulting positioning in §1, because it is precisely the mistake a practitioner
would not make. Mechanism: eligibility gating in `analyzer.py` runs at scoring
time on fields the model self-reports (`applicant_type_match`, `trl_match`,
`geography_match`) rather than as a hard filter before an item can be
recommended.

**3. A quarter of recommendations carry a link a reader cannot use.**
(§9a Q3 — traceable)
Structural precision 0.76, essentially all of it broken links, plus a further
23% of all items pointing at a funder homepage rather than a specific programme.
"Application links are verified" is a claim on the landing page. Mechanism: link
resolution and verification in `analyzer.py`; `link_status` is already recorded,
so the pipeline knows the link is broken and recommends it anyway.

**4. The shortlist is half the advertised size.** (§9a Q8)
4–5 scored recommendations against `SHORTLIST_SIZE = 10`, with 16.8 watchlist
items alongside. The sample report shows the same shape (4 scored, 21 watchlist)
and is already flagged in `PROJECT_LOG.md`. Either the promise or the routing is
wrong; both are defensible, but the mismatch is not.

**5. Retrieval churns.** (§9a Q5 as a diagnostic)
0.21 exact-name agreement between runs of the same company, with no programme
appearing in every run. Not a finding to act on directly — it is the symptom
whose causes are findings 2, 3 and 4.

## Proposals awaiting decision

Nothing here gets built until Thomas says which to proceed with. All four are
output-quality changes, so all four are his call.

**1. Make eligibility a hard gate rather than a score input.** *(Recommended)*
- **Problem:** items the pipeline itself has flagged as TRL, geography or
  applicant-type mismatches can still be recommended.
- **Evidence:** exclusion accuracy 0.90 (n=5) / 0.84 (n=16); two runs recommended
  a programme whose own recorded `trl_match` was `false`.
- **Harms:** Q1, and §9a failure mode 1 — the most damaging one.
- **Mechanism:** scoring and routing in `analyzer.py`; the fields already exist.
- **Options:** (a) hard-exclude any item with a recorded mismatch from the main
  list, routing it to the watchlist with the reason — small change, low risk,
  fixes the cause; (b) require a stated eligibility justification per item and
  drop items that can't produce one — larger, higher token cost, catches more
  than the self-reported fields do; (c) leave it and rely on the reader.
- **Recommendation:** (a) now, (b) considered only if the next cycle shows (a)
  didn't move the number.
- **Verification:** exclusion accuracy ≥ 0.98 across the next cycle's runs; zero
  `must_not_appear` violations.

**2. Don't recommend a programme whose link is known broken.** *(Recommended)*
- **Problem:** links already verified as broken are shipped to users anyway.
- **Evidence:** structural precision 0.76, broken-link rate 0.11 across all
  items; the landing page claims links are verified.
- **Harms:** Q3, and the credibility test directly.
- **Mechanism:** link resolution in `analyzer.py`; `link_status` is recorded but
  not acted on.
- **Options:** (a) demote any item with a broken link to the watchlist and say
  why; (b) retry resolution against the funder's site before demoting — better
  recall, more calls, more cost; (c) show the item with a visible "link
  unverified" marker and let the reader judge.
- **Recommendation:** (b) then (a) — retry once, demote if it still fails.
- **Verification:** broken-link rate below 0.02; specific-link rate above 0.85.

**3. Close the gap between 10 promised and 4–5 delivered.**
- **Problem:** the pipeline promises ten scored opportunities and returns four.
- **Evidence:** 4–5 main against `SHORTLIST_SIZE = 10`, with 16.8 watchlist items.
- **Harms:** Q8, and the sample report is the first thing a visitor sees.
- **Mechanism:** the watchlist routing rules and `_shortlist_key` in `analyzer.py`.
- **Options:** (a) loosen watchlist routing so partner-route items with a real
  direct path stay in the main list; (b) lower the promise to match reality; (c)
  widen discovery so more genuinely-direct candidates reach scoring — most
  expensive, most likely to help Q6 too.
- **Recommendation:** diagnose before choosing. The next cycle's real-run data
  will show whether this is specific to multi-geography hardware profiles or
  general, and the answer changes which option is right.
- **Verification:** main-list count median ≥ 8 with structural precision holding
  at or above its current level — volume bought by lowering the bar is not a win.

**4. Widen the ground-truth bench beyond two companies.**
- **Problem:** recall and exclusion accuracy rest on two companies in one sector,
  so they are directional at best.
- **Evidence:** `eval/cases.py`; one real company and one fictional.
- **Harms:** the measurement, not the product — but every proposal above is
  verified against it.
- **Options:** (a) add three cases across NZ/AU and software/deeptech, researched
  properly — costs research time, and wrong ground truth is worse than none;
  (b) leave it and lean on structural measures from real runs once traffic
  exists; (c) both.
- **Recommendation:** (b) until the soft launch happens. Real users on real
  companies are better evidence than more synthetic cases, and the soft launch
  should supply them within the fortnight.
- **Verification:** ≥5 real completed runs readable in the next cycle.
